# Divergence Response — Act II

**Due:** Thu Oct 22, 2026 23:59 MT · **Points:** 50

## What to do

Commit `journey/divergence/act-2.md` to your repo covering your section's first weeks of generator runs, .

For each divergence your section produced:

1. **What the report said** — the vector, the agreement score, the diagnosis.
2. **Which defect it was.** Builds disagreeing with each other is **ambiguous prose** and it is yours to fix. Builds agreeing but missing your vector is a **wrong vector** — the engine is self-consistent and your test was wrong.
3. **What you changed**, and the sentence or vector you changed it in.
4. **What happened next.** Did agreement improve? Did it stay improved?

Then: your section's score at the start of this window and at the end.

## How this is graded

On **outcome**, not activity. A revision that did not move the score is worth less than one that did, and a divergence left unaddressed while the class was blocked is worth nothing.

Diagnosing correctly matters too. Editing a vector to match the behavior when the prose was at fault fixes the symptom and leaves the ambiguity in place.

## If your section never diverged

Say so, and say why you think it held — which sentences you pinned early, and what you would have gotten wrong if you had not. That is a legitimate and useful answer.

## How this is graded

**Push to your repository.** The autograder runs on the push and posts its
feedback as a **GitHub issue** on that repo, scored against the rubric below.
Read the issue; that is where your feedback lives.

There is nothing to submit in Canvas. Your commit history *is* the submission,
and the commit timestamp is what the late policy measures.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every divergence in the window addressed.
- Diagnosis stated and correct for each.
- Before and after scores given.
